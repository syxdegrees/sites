## Subject Direction
- Show clients who have achieved the result your service delivers — not the process, not the work itself
- Ideal: a real or stock client in a "after" moment (speaking on stage, holding the finished product, in their transformed space)
- If multiple client avatars (age, gender, background), use multiple images to show range — see dual-image hero wireframe variant
- Never show the business owner or team in the hero image

## Style
- Mood: confident, aspirational, warm — conveys "this could be you"
- Composition: lifestyle or environmental — person in context, not posed headshot
- Background: contextual (stage, office, outdoor space) with natural depth of field

## Technical
- Aspect ratios: wide landscape (16:9 or 3:1) for full-width hero; can crop to 4:3 for contained hero
- Overlay treatment: none preferred; if text overlays image use dark overlay at 40–60% opacity to maintain readability
- Avoid closeup faces when using text-on-image layouts — face placement conflicts with text as viewport changes

## Do / Don't
- Do: show the client outcome visually (book in hand, speaking on stage, finished project)
- Do: use images with good tonal range so stat badge and results bar remain readable
- Do: choose images where the subject is off-center or looking inward so text zones don't conflict
- Don't: use the business owner or team as the hero image subject
- Don't: use stock photos of generic "business people" with no connection to the specific result
- Don't: use a closeup portrait as the hero if any text will overlay the image
- Don't: use busy or high-contrast backgrounds without a dark overlay
